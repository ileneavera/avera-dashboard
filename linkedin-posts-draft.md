## Post 2: The Myth-Buster (Add value + attract ICP)

**Theme:** M&A misconceptions for founders

```
Most founders think M&A is only for big companies with big budgets.

That's wrong.

Some of the most impactful acquisitions I've worked on were under $50M. A few were under $10M. The best ones weren't about size — they were about timing and fit.

Here's what I've seen work:

1. Acquiring a team that would take 18 months to hire
2. Buying a product that fills a gap your customers are already asking about
3. Entering a new market through a company that already has traction there

You don't need a $500M war chest. You need a clear thesis and someone who knows how to execute.

If you're a founder sitting on Series B or C capital and wondering whether M&A could accelerate your roadmap — it probably can. Happy to share what I've seen work.
```

## Post 3: The Behind-the-Scenes (Show expertise + build trust)

**Theme:** What actually kills deals

```
I've worked on deals that closed in 6 weeks and deals that dragged on for 9 months.

The difference was almost never the financials.

The three things that kill deals most often:

1. No internal champion. If the founder isn't driving the process, it stalls. Every time. Delegating to someone who doesn't have authority to make decisions is the fastest way to lose a target.

2. Diligence paralysis. Teams that try to answer every question before making a decision end up answering none of them. The best acquirers know which risks matter and which ones don't.

3. Cultural mismatch that nobody talks about. You can model synergies all day. But if the target's team doesn't want to be there post-close, you bought a shell.

M&A isn't just finance. It's judgment, speed, and people. That's what I spend my time on.
```
