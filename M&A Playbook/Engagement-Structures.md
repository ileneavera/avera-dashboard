# Engagement Structures
### Avera M&A Playbook

How to scope, price, and structure fractional M&A advisory engagements — from the first conversation through the SOW. Includes how to handle referral-fee traps, pricing frameworks, and client red flags.

*Last updated: March 2026*

---

## The Core Problem to Solve

Most founders and VCs have never hired fractional corp dev before. They default to two mental models:
1. **Investment banker** — takes a success fee (Lehman formula) on close
2. **Full-time hire** — salaried employee with full context

Neither is right for Avera. The pitch is a third model: **senior operator, project-based or retainer, engaged at the level the company actually needs**. The job is to educate the client on what that looks like before they try to force you into one of the other boxes.

## Engagement Models

### 1. Project-Based / Flat Fee
**What it is:** A defined deliverable for a fixed price.

**Best for:**
- Term sheet review and markup
- Data room audit
- Buyer list development
- CIM review or financial model QA
- One-time strategic assessment ("should we be buying or being bought?")

**Pricing approach:**
- Price based on estimated hours × your effective hourly rate, plus a small buffer for scope creep
- Be explicit about what's included and what triggers a new project
- Works well as an entry point — low commitment for the client, clear value delivered

**Example scopes:**
- *Term sheet review + annotated memo:* $3,000–$6,000 flat
- *Data room audit + gap report:* $4,000–$8,000 flat
- *Buyer universe + outreach strategy:* $5,000–$10,000 flat

---

### 2. Monthly Retainer
**What it is:** A fixed monthly fee for ongoing advisory availability — typically a defined number of hours per week/month.

**Best for:**
- Companies actively running a process who need consistent support
- Founders who want a trusted advisor on-call through a multi-month process
- Situations where the scope is clear but the timeline is uncertain

**Pricing approach:**
- Anchor on time commitment: 10 hrs/month vs. 20 hrs/month vs. 40+ hrs/month
- Typical range: $5,000–$15,000/month depending on intensity and seniority required
- Set a minimum engagement length (3 months is standard) — protects against getting pulled in and then disengaged before value is delivered
- Define what's included: calls, document review, meetings, travel (if any)

**What to specify in the SOW:**
- Hours per month (hard cap or soft expectation)
- Communication norms (async first? scheduled calls? response time SLA?)
- What requires a new SOW (e.g., attending in-person negotiations is out of scope at base retainer)

---

### 3. Retainer + Success Fee
**What it is:** A monthly retainer during the process + a success fee paid at close, typically a % of deal value.

**Best for:**
- Full sell-side process engagements where Ilene is deeply embedded
- When the client wants to align incentives on close (which can be good — but watch for the trap below)
- Longer engagements where pure retainer gets expensive for the client

**Pricing approach:**
- Retainer: lower than pure retainer model (they're getting future upside) — e.g., $5,000–$8,000/month
- Success fee: typically 0.5–2% of deal value for fractional advisors (bankers charge more; this should be positioned as significantly below banker economics)
- Success fee cap: consider a cap so it doesn't become disproportionate if the deal is large
- Threshold: define what "close" means (signing? funding? all conditions met?)

**Why this is good:** Aligns incentives, reduces client's upfront cash burn, and ties Ilene's comp to the outcome she's working toward.

**The watch-out:** Don't let the success fee become the *only* compensation. If the deal falls apart for reasons outside your control (buyer walks, board disagrees), you've done months of work for nothing. The retainer protects against this.

---

### 4. Hourly / Advisory
**What it is:** Time billed at an hourly rate, typically used for light-touch advisory relationships.

**Best for:**
- Clients who need occasional guidance but don't have a current process
- Board members or investors who want a resource for their portfolio
- Expert calls or one-off consultations

**Pricing approach:**
- Effective rate: $300–$600/hour depending on complexity and context
- Best for <10 hours/month — beyond that, retainer is more efficient for both parties
- Require a minimum (e.g., 3-hour minimum billing) to avoid micro-engagements

**When to offer it:** Good relationship-building tool (easier for someone to say yes to 2 hours than a full retainer), but be deliberate — don't build a practice of $500 advisory calls.

---

### 5. Success Fee Only (The Referral Fee Trap)
**What it is:** No upfront compensation; Ilene gets paid only if a deal closes.

**When it's offered:** Almost always by:
- Founders who don't have budget or are unsure of the value
- People who've confused you with a finder or broker
- Companies that want "motivation" alignment but don't want to pay for your time

**The problem:**
- You take on all the execution risk — if the deal falls through for any reason, you worked for free
- It changes the dynamic: you become dependent on the deal closing, which can distort judgment (e.g., you push too hard toward close even when slowing down would be better for the client)
- It devalues your expertise: if you can only get paid on outcomes, why should a client respect your judgment?
- Fractional corp dev is fundamentally different from a finder/broker — you're providing an ongoing service, not a one-time introduction

**How to respond when offered:**
> "I appreciate you thinking about alignment, and I'm happy to include a success component. But I don't do pure success-fee arrangements — the work I do throughout the process (TS review, diligence management, negotiations) has value regardless of outcome, and that needs to be compensated. What I can do is a reduced retainer with a meaningful success fee at close — that way we're both bought in and you're protected from paying full advisory rates if the deal doesn't get there."

**When a success fee alone might make sense:**
- You're genuinely making a single introduction with no ongoing work involved (treat it like a finder's fee, not an advisory engagement — clarify that in writing)
- Very small deals where a flat/hourly arrangement would cost more than the deal economics support
- You know and trust the client deeply and are doing it as a relationship investment

**The rule:** Any deal where you're doing real work over weeks or months needs a retainer component. Period.

## Scoping a New Engagement — Process

### Step 1: Discovery Call
Before any proposal, understand:
- What exactly do they need help with? (TS review vs. full process vs. ongoing corp dev)
- What's the timeline? (Urgency affects pricing and structure)
- Who else is involved? (Lawyers, bankers, other advisors)
- What does their internal team look like? (Who will you actually work with?)
- What's the budget expectation? (Save time — ask early)
- What does success look like to them?

**Don't skip the budget question.** Framing: *"Before I put together a proposal, it would help to know if you have a rough budget in mind so I can tailor the structure to what makes sense for you."*

### Step 2: Scoping Document (Internal)
Before writing the proposal, write down:
- What's the actual scope? (Be specific)
- What's out of scope? (Be explicit — scope creep is the #1 issue in fractional work)
- Estimated hours per month / per deliverable
- Your proposed engagement model and rationale
- Any dependencies or assumptions (e.g., "assumes data room is already set up")

### Step 3: The Proposal / SOW
Keep it clean and simple. Should include:
1. **Engagement overview** — what you're being engaged to do (1–2 paragraphs)
2. **Scope of work** — bullet list of specific deliverables or activities included
3. **Out of scope** — what's explicitly not included
4. **Engagement model** — project/retainer/retainer + success fee, with amounts
5. **Payment terms** — net 15 or net 30; first payment due on signing
6. **Term** — start date, minimum term, notice period for termination
7. **Expenses** — who pays for travel, software, etc. (if relevant)
8. **Confidentiality** — brief statement
9. **Signature block**

**Length:** Keep it to 1–2 pages. Long SOWs signal over-engineering and slow down signing.

## Pricing Framework by Engagement Type

### How to Set Your Rate
Anchor on the value delivered, not hours spent. Ilene closing or protecting $5M in deal value is worth far more than $500/hr × 40 hours.

Factors that justify higher fees:
- Company stage (later stage = more complex = higher fee)
- Deal size (larger deal = more at stake = higher fee)
- Urgency (compressed timeline = premium)
- Complexity (multiple buyers, cross-border, regulatory issues)
- Exclusivity (if she can't take other clients during the engagement)

Factors that might adjust down:
- Smaller scope (TS review only vs. full process)
- Relationship investment (strategic client who will refer others)
- Equity component (taking some compensation in equity is an option for longer engagements with aligned upside)

### Rough Benchmarks by Engagement Type

| Engagement | Structure | Range |
|---|---|---|
| One-time TS review | Flat fee | $3,000–$8,000 |
| Data room audit | Flat fee | $4,000–$8,000 |
| Buyer list + outreach strategy | Flat fee | $5,000–$12,000 |
| Light retainer (advisory, 10 hrs/mo) | Monthly | $5,000–$8,000/mo |
| Active process support (20 hrs/mo) | Monthly | $8,000–$15,000/mo |
| Full sell-side process (deeply embedded) | Retainer + success fee | $8,000–$12,000/mo + 0.5–1.5% of deal |
| On-call hourly advisory | Hourly | $350–$550/hr |

*These are starting points. Adjust based on deal complexity, company size, and your capacity.*

### Success Fee Benchmarks
- Traditional M&A bankers: Lehman formula (5% on first $1M, 4% on next $1M, etc.) or flat 2–5% for mid-market
- Fractional advisors: typically 0.5–2% of deal value
- At $10M deal: 1% = $100K success fee
- At $50M deal: 0.5–0.75% = $250–375K success fee
- Consider a cap above a certain deal value threshold

## Red Flags — When to Walk Away

**Budget mismatch:**
- "We just want to pick your brain" = they want free consulting
- "We can pay you when the deal closes" (on a 6-month process) = success-fee trap
- "Our budget is $X" (where X is <50% of your minimum) = walk away or re-scope aggressively

**Unclear decision-maker:**
- Can't get a clear answer on who signs the check and who has authority to engage
- Multiple stakeholders with conflicting views on what they need
- "Let me check with my partner/board/investor" after 3 conversations

**Unrealistic expectations:**
- "We need a deal in 60 days" (without any preparation done)
- "We already have a buyer, we just need you to rubber stamp this" (but want full advisory engagement)
- "We've talked to 5 advisors and no one has the right approach" (probably a difficult client)

**Process disrespect:**
- Slow to respond, disorganized, or doesn't follow through on commitments during the sales process — that's a preview of the engagement
- Nickel-and-diming on the proposal before you've even started

**Misaligned incentives:**
- Founder wants to sell; investors don't (or vice versa)
- Board has unrealistic price expectations that can't be met in the current market
- Someone on the team knows about the deal who shouldn't

**Red flag ≠ automatic no.** Some of these are navigable with clear conversation upfront. But go in with eyes open.

## Client Segmentation — How to Think About Prioritizing

Not all engagements are created equal. Rough tiers:

| Client Type | What They Need | Engagement Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Series B–D founder with M&A mandate | Full corp dev capability | Full retainer or retainer + success fee |
| VC with portfolio companies to support | On-call expert for multiple portfolio cos | Retainer with GC firm, or deal-by-deal project |
| Company with inbound offer, no process | TS review + deal management | Project-based → retainer as scope expands |
| Company exploring whether to sell | Strategic assessment | Flat fee project; upsell to full process |
| Early-stage founder (pre-Series B) | Partnership and biz dev help | Partnerships engagement or decline (not core ICP) |

**Highest-value clients:** VC firms and family offices who can refer multiple portfolio companies over time. One GP relationship > ten individual founder relationships.

## Lessons Learned — Add Over Time

- **March 2026:** "Second eyes" framing from VCs is a common entry point. Start with a project (TS review, data room audit) rather than pitching a full engagement. Earn the larger scope.
- **March 2026:** Referral-fee-only offers are common when clients don't understand fractional vs. finder. The best response isn't to say no — it's to reframe as retainer + success fee, which gives them the alignment they want without putting all risk on you.
